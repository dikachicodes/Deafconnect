DeafConnect

A space built for Deaf & Hard-of-Hearing communities

DeafConnect provides fully captioned media, visual-only notifications, and accessible services — designed from the ground up to WCAG 2.2 Level AA standards. No audio required, ever.

What we offer

Captioned media

Every video carries synchronised WebVTT captions and a full text transcript. No audio required to understand any content.

Visual alerts only

All system notifications — confirmations, errors, warnings — are delivered as colour-coded visual banners, never audio tones.

Accessible booking

Book BSL interpreter services, audiology appointments, and community sessions with full keyboard navigation support.

Text messaging

Send and receive messages with visual delivery badges. No audio confirmation sounds at any point in the flow.

WCAG 2.2 AA

This platform is built to Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2 Level AA — verified through Lighthouse and axe-DevTools automated auditing plus manual WCAG checklist review.

About DeafConnect

DeafConnect is a research prototype demonstrating that accessible web design is not a trade-off — it is better design for everyone. The platform implements WCAG 2.2 Level AA standards from inception, informed by the Social Model of Disability, Universal Design theory, and the Technology Acceptance Model.

Design principles

Visual-first communication

Every system message — confirmations, errors, warnings — is communicated visually. No interaction point requires audio.

Caption quality, not just presence

Captions are synchronised, high-contrast, and resize-safe. A poorly timed or unreadable caption is as inaccessible as no caption.

Keyboard accessibility throughout

Every interactive element — forms, media controls, navigation, messaging — is fully operable by keyboard alone.

Standards-verified, task-evaluated

WCAG compliance is verified by Lighthouse and axe-DevTools. Real task-based evaluation measures actual usability outcomes.

Theoretical grounding

Social Model of Disability

Barriers to participation are created by design decisions, not by users' hearing status. This platform removes those barriers through design (Lawson & Beckett, 2020).

Universal Design

Systems designed for the widest range of users produce better products for all. Accessibility and usability are complementary, not competing (Hedvall et al., 2025).

Technology Acceptance Model

Perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness drive adoption. Reducing cognitive effort for DHH users increases engagement and satisfaction (Harsanto et al., 2023).
